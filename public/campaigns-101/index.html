Campaigns 101 / short course

    A short course for creative studios · 01

    What is
    a campaign?

    Learn what a campaign is, why we make one and how the team works on it.

    By the end, you should be able to:

    Explain a campaign in one sentence, connect an idea to different assets, and ask the right questions before production.

    12 short views · About 10 minutes, plus discussion · No previous marketing knowledge needed.

    Use ← → to present, Contents to jump, or Read all to scroll. A real case study, illustrated teaching examples and a quick check are included.

    Lesson 1 · 02

    A campaign is a plan to get a message to people for a clear reason.

    We choose who we want to reach, what we want to say, where to say it and when. The work follows that plan.

    WHY / WHO

    Goal + people

    What should people understand, feel or do? Who are we trying to reach?

    WHAT / HOW

    Idea + work

    What connects the films, pictures, words or activities we make?

    WHERE / WHEN

    Place + timing

    Where will people see it? When will we show it? How will we check the result?

    A simple example

    A bakery wants people nearby to try its new bread. It shows the bread in short videos, puts a sign outside and offers samples that week. Each part helps the same goal.

    A campaign can be small, use unpaid posts or run in just one place. There is no fixed number of pieces to make. Reference: Mailchimp’s campaign-planning guide ↗

    Lesson 2 · 03

    Why make a campaign?

    Help people understand

    Show what a product is, what makes it useful or why someone might want it.

    Help people remember

    Use a clear idea across the work so people can recognise the brand and message.

    Help the team choose

    Use the goal to decide which shots, words and pictures belong in the work.

    TermMeaningSimple example
    BrandHow people recognise a business and what they think of it.The bakery’s name, look and reputation.
    CampaignA planned effort with a clear goal.A week of inviting people to try the new bread.
    Asset / adOne piece of work. An ad is a piece made to advertise.One video or poster in that campaign.

    A campaign can help people notice, understand, buy or return. Results also depend on the product, offer and how the work reaches people.

    Lesson 3 · 04

    Who is it for? What should happen?

    Before making the film, decide what you want it to do.

    Made-up example for this course

    DAY reusable bottle

    Audience: people who travel to work.

    What we want to show: the bottle as part of an ordinary day.

    Possible action: visit the product page to inspect it.

    Three questions first

    • Who are we trying to reach?
    • What do they need to know?
    • What should they do next?

    Check what the product does and what those people care about.

    The goal affects the work.

    If we want people to remember the brand, make it easy to recognise. If we want them to understand a feature, show it clearly. If we want them to buy, give them a reason and a way to buy.

    Agree a timeframe and a relevant success measure before launch. Reference: begin with measurable goals ↗

    Lesson 4 · 05

    One idea. Different ways to show it.

    The idea connects the work. The film, poster and page each show that idea in their own way.

    Show the same bottle through an ordinary day.

    A proposed idea for our made-up bottle. We would check the real product before making claims about it.

    A film

    Follow the same bottle through an ordinary morning, commute and workday.

    A poster

    Compose the bottle beside familiar everyday belongings.

    A page

    Explain the product clearly, using the same visual character and verified details.

    A useful test

    Can you explain how each piece shows the idea? Keep the message and look connected, while giving each piece its own job.

    Where do references fit?

    Use a reference to identify a specific principle—composition, pacing, material treatment or storytelling. Write why that principle serves this audience and idea. The new work needs its own product truth and direction.

    Case study · 06

    “Share a Coke”: an idea people could take part in.

    In Australia in 2011, Coca-Cola wanted a closer connection with young adults. It invited people to share a Coke with someone.

    CENTRAL INVITATIONShare a Coke
    with someone.

    On the pack

    People’s names appeared on bottles and cans.

    Online

    People could send a virtual Coke through Facebook.

    In person

    Travelling kiosks let people customise cans.

    What we can learn

    The bottle, online activity and kiosk gave people different ways to take part in the same idea. That connection makes this a useful example of a campaign.

    Lesson 5 · 07

    What will we make? Where will people see it?

    Asset: a piece of work, such as a film or picture. Channel: where we show it. CTA: the call to action—the next step we ask people to take.

    Fictional DAY assetIts jobPossible placement
    Short filmIntroduce the everyday-journey idea.Social feed or campaign page.
    Hero imageMake the bottle recognisable at a glance.Poster or page opening.
    Detail imagesHelp people inspect verified product features.Product page or a sequence of social images.
    Campaign pageConnect the idea to information and a next step.Website reached from the campaign.
    Before locking the storyboard

    Choose where the work will appear. A phone feed, large poster and product page need different framing, text and timing. A film should also make sense when the sound is off.

    Can a frame from the film become a still?

    Yes, when its composition, resolution, readability and message suit the intended placement. Plan important still compositions deliberately. Reuse does not remove the need to check quality.

    Lesson 6 · 08

    From brief to audience.

    PlanGoal, audience, research and scope.
    →
    DevelopIdea, board and styleframes.
    →
    ProduceFeasibility tests, execution and review.
    →
    ReleaseDelivery, distribution and learning.

    Creative director

    Sets the idea and what the audience should understand. Approves the creative direction.

    Art director

    Plans the look: framing, lighting, storyboard and key images that show how the work should feel.

    Production / CGI lead

    Works out how to make it, builds or coordinates the scenes, and checks what is possible.

    The client or marketing team agrees the goal and where the campaign will run. A producer manages time, workload and reviews. One person can do more than one role; everyone should know who makes the final decision.

    Lesson 7 · 09

    Give feedback people can use.

    01 →

    Make

    Show a version and state what needs review.

    02 →

    Review

    Compare it with the agreed goal and direction.

    03 →

    Resolve

    Agree the changes and who will make them.

    04

    Revise

    Check that the change solves the problem.

    Needs work → repeat the focused review   |   Approved → next stage

    A broad note

    “Make it more premium.”

    A useful direction

    “The background competes with the bottle, so its shape is hard to read. Give the product a clearer silhouette and more breathing space.”

    Approve at the right level.

    Agree the idea before detailed production; agree the board and look before final execution. Record the version and decision. Changes to approved work need a time and scope check.

    Lesson 8 · 10

    How can it grow? Did it work?

    Grow the campaign

    Another format: make a shorter edit or a different crop.

    Another message: show a feature or a different use.

    Another audience: check what those people need, then change the work to suit them.

    Keep the idea and product facts clear. Agree the extra work, time and cost.

    Check the result

    Want people to remember? Check what they recognise or remember.

    Want people to understand? Ask what they understood.

    Want people to act? Count the agreed actions, such as enquiries or purchases.

    Views and likes can help you understand the result. They do not answer every question.

    Reference: Google’s guide to objectives, KPIs and supporting metrics (PDF) ↗

    What is a KPI?

    It means “key performance indicator”: the main number or measure used to check the goal. Choose it before launch. Compare results with a suitable starting point. More sales after a campaign does not, by itself, prove the campaign caused the increase.

    Put it into practice · 11

    Try two questions.

    Choose an answer. Then use the starter brief to describe a small campaign of your own.

    1. What makes three posters a campaign?

    Read the explanation

    A shared goal, idea and audience within a plan connects the posters. Matching dimensions is only a format choice.

    2. We want people to understand the product. What should we check?

    Read the explanation

    Ask what people understood. Likes alone do not tell us that.

    A one-page campaign starter

    Purpose: what should change?
    Audience: who should respond?
    Evidence: what do we know and still need to verify?
    Idea: what connects the work?
    Outputs: what does each asset contribute?
    Distribution: where, when and toward what next step?
    Production: owners, scope, schedule and approvals?
    Success: what evidence will we review?
