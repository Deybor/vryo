    01 / The Odu brief

    Independent portfolio concept

    A quiet
    foundation.

    A film and still-image campaign for Odu, the solid wood coffee table by Majeurs.

    Begin with its distinctive legs.
    End with its calm place in a home.

    Agreed audience priority + reveal directionProposed idea wording + outputsLater product checks + production plan

    For developing the storyboard and look. This is VYRO’s independent concept, with Majeurs credited as the product maker. It is not a commissioned or approved Majeurs campaign.

    Say this

    “We want the viewer to discover what makes this table distinctive, then see why it belongs in a home. Today we’re agreeing the creative direction and the first visual work.”

    02 / The job and audience

    Show what VYRO can do
    for a furniture brand.

    Make Odu’s form, wood and room fit clear. Then explain the decisions behind the images in our portfolio.

    Primary audience · agreed priority

    Potential VYRO clients

    Furniture-brand owners, marketing teams and design leads are our working audience. They should see that we can carry one idea through a film, stills and a clear case study.

    Desired response: “I can see what this studio could do for our product.” Consider a conversation with VYRO.

    Secondary audience · agreed inclusion

    Potential Odu buyers

    People choosing furniture for a home or interior project are our working audience. They should recognise Odu, appreciate the wood and imagine how it fits in a room.

    Desired response: “I understand this table, and I can picture living with it.”

    What we can judge now: does the work communicate those things? Enquiries, reach and sales need a later publishing and measurement plan.

    The audience priority is agreed. These descriptions are working assumptions; detailed buyer research is still to come.

    Ask the team

    “If a furniture-brand client sees this, what should they understand about our thinking as well as our rendering?”

    The natural grain and thickness give the surface character.

    2

    One opening composition can begin with this restrained vertical shape.

    3

    The photograph shows different visible faces of the two supports. Verify their exact orientation before building production geometry.

    Majeurs describes Odu as solid wood, low in profile and calm in presence, handcrafted in Lagos to commission. Those qualities give the campaign its starting point.

    Product facts and photograph: Majeurs · Odu. Wood species, exact finish, dimensions and construction will be checked in the later product-research stage.

    Show the photograph

    “Look at the difference between the narrow edge and the broad face. That’s a feature we can explore. We need to preserve the actual table as we do it.”

    04 / The central idea · working wording

    Odu is the quiet foundation
    of everyday life.

    The table stays grounded. Light and framing gradually reveal its form and the room around it.

    Product meaningWhat we do with it
    RootednessKeep contact with the floor believable. Let the broad supports feel settled.
    Honest materialShow grain, thickness and finish clearly. Keep the same wood identity across images.
    Calm authorityUse careful framing, breathing space and measured reveals.
    A place in daily lifeResolve the film into a room where its low height and comfortable fit are clear.

    Each reveal should help us understand
    why this table belongs in a home.

    This is a direction for the work, not a locked advertising line. Majeurs’ explanation of the name Odu informs the idea; its cultural interpretation is attributed to the brand.

    Majeurs’ product description → our creative interpretation above.

    Say this

    “Quiet doesn’t mean nothing happens. Each change gives us something useful to notice. The room at the end explains the presence we felt at the beginning.”

    05 / The reveal story · agreed direction

    From a leg detail
    to a place in life.

    Five stages of understanding. The art director decides how shots connect them.

    01 / Notice a detail

    Show
    A selective leg edge or plane, with enough shadow or floor contact to feel grounded.
    Communicate
    Curiosity about a distinctive, tangible form.
    Light & connection
    Make the chosen edge readable. Leave enough orientation to connect it to the next view.

    01 / Notice a detail

    Show
    A selective leg edge or plane, with enough shadow or floor contact to feel grounded.
    Communicate
    Curiosity about a distinctive, tangible form.
    Light & connection
    Make the chosen edge readable. Leave enough orientation to connect it to the next view.

    02 / Discover its architectural form

    Show
    Narrow edges, broad wooden faces and the space beneath the top. Introduce selected floor, wall or shadow clues.
    Communicate
    The distinctive composition of Odu’s supports.
    Light & connection
    Bring a broad face or space into attention. Connect it to the detail we have just seen.

    03 / Appreciate the material

    Show
    Grain, finish and thickness across a leg or tabletop detail.
    Communicate
    Warmth, tactile character and material honesty.
    Light & connection
    Draw attention to surface and grain. Preserve the same wood identity as the earlier frames.

    04 / Understand the structure

    Show
    Wider framing connects the legs, thick top and floor into a complete table.
    Communicate
    Recognition, grounded proportions and a sense of stability.
    Light & connection
    Clarify the relationships between parts. A wider view makes their function readable.

    05 / See its place in life

    Show
    The table beside correctly scaled furniture, with a restrained sign of use. Hold a hero composition with product identification.
    Communicate
    Calm belonging and a clearly low profile.
    Light & connection
    Let the room become readable without overwhelming the wood or hiding the leg form. Resolve the early spatial clues.

    One connected discovery: leg edge → broad face → grain → complete structure → room. Wood is present from the beginning; stage three draws our attention to it.

    These are attention beats, not five required shots or fixed time slots. A shot can carry more than one beat. Keep enough orientation in the opening; test an earlier contextual glimpse if viewers feel lost.

    Use the five buttons

    “Form asks us to appreciate the shape. Structure helps us understand how the legs and top relate as a table. The final room connects both to everyday life.”

    06 / Three agreed refinements

    Make the reveals belong together.

    1. 01

      Repeat a feature that belongs to Odu.

      Use the relationship between a narrow edge, a broad wooden face and the space beneath the top. Develop the actual supports into recognisable compositions.

    2. 02

      Let the room appear in small clues.

      Floor, wall planes, lines or shadows can enter the early frames. When we see the room, those earlier clues should make sense. The exact environment is open.

    3. 03

      Give every light change a job.

      Reveal an edge, a surface, grain or the connection to the top. Keep the table’s finish and shape consistent as the light changes.

    Review question: does the next image add understanding of the same table in the same world?

    Ask the team

    “What will each reveal help us discover? Which early clue will become clear in the final room?”

    08 / The final room

    Let Odu fit beautifully
    and calmly.

    Its low profile must be clear beside the other furniture. The complete room is part of the product story.

    Build the relationship.

    Use verified product dimensions and correctly scaled surrounding furniture. Show enough of the floor and seating to judge the table’s height and comfortable place in the room.

    Keep breathing space around the table. Let one restrained sign of use help it feel lived with.

    Check the image.

    Does the table read as low beside the sofa? Are its legs and thick top still readable? Do camera height and lens choice preserve its proportions?

    Does the room resolve the earlier planes, floor and shadows? Does the table remain the visual focus?

    Early framesSelected spatial clues→Final roomThose clues become a home→Final holdOdu + clear product identification

    Exact room, props, camera and lighting treatments are for art-direction development. A grain close-up can show material character; Lagos provenance or handcraft needs attributed copy or genuine making evidence.

    Say this

    “The final frame has to make its low height easy to see. The setting should give the table a place, with the furniture around it helping us understand its proportions.”

    10 / First visual-development handoff

    Develop the board
    and three key styleframes.

    Show how the story works and how it should feel. Explain your visual decisions so the team can build from them.

    The storyboard

    Cover the whole reveal, including the final hold. Use enough frames to explain changes within a shot, movements and transitions.

    Show which detail connects to which wider view. Develop a timed animatic once the main sequence is clear.

    Three styleframes

    Test the opening leg composition, a material or structure view, and the final room relationship. The AD chooses the exact moments.

    These test the look: composition, wood, light, contrast and setting. They do not replace the board or the timing test.

    Write this beside each board frame.Include start / end frames when needed.
    Purpose
    What does the viewer discover here?
    Framing & movement
    Viewpoint, crop, camera movement or hold; what stays still?
    Lighting intention
    What becomes visible, and why? Include source or motivation, shadow behaviour and a useful reference.
    Connection & timing
    How do we enter and leave the shot? Give a rough duration to test.
    Sound, text & format
    Any sound cue or copy; intended framing and space for product identification.
    Open question
    What product fact or technical assumption needs checking?

    Voiceover is not required. Sound, final copy and logo use remain open. See the studio storyboard example for sheet structure.

    Ask the art director

    “Can you take us from the opening leg detail to the room without losing the table’s identity? Show us the key light and framing decisions that make that possible.”

    11 / Team responsibilities

    A clear direction.
    A clear handoff.

    Creative director

    Purpose and idea

    Explain the job, audience and desired takeaway. Keep the campaign consistent. Review the board and look against this brief, then approve the creative direction.

    Art director

    Visual decisions

    Develop framing, setting, colour, lighting intention, references and transitions. Lead the storyboard and styleframes, then guide and review the visual work.

    CGI lead

    Feasibility and execution

    Flag technical questions early. Later, turn the developed direction into assets, tasks, tests and a production plan. Coordinate the named makers and check the build.

    Lighting: the AD defines what the light needs to communicate. A named lighting artist builds and tests the setup with AD review. The CGI lead can take that role if assigned.

    One person may hold more than one role. Record who owns each task and decision. Odu-specific dates, review rounds and final production sign-off will be agreed with the production plan.

    Say this

    “We need the CGI lead’s input early on what might be difficult. The AD still develops the visual intention. We’ll name who executes lighting when we assign the production work.”

    12 / Review the board and look

    Judge it against the story.

    1. 01

      Do Odu’s distinctive legs lead the opening? Can we orient ourselves?

    2. 02

      Does every reveal add useful information? Does the sequence connect?

    3. 03

      Do the wood, geometry and floor relationship stay believable?

    4. 04

      Does the final room resolve the early clues and show the table’s low profile?

    5. 05

      Can the hero and detail stills carry the same idea as complete images?

    6. 06

      Would a potential VYRO client understand the thinking behind this work?

    An example of useful feedback

    “The final room needs to make the low profile clearer. Please test a view that includes more of the sofa and floor. Keep the quiet composition.”

    Name the problem, the desired result and what should be preserved. Let the maker propose a solution.

    AD gathers visual notes; CD resolves changes to the idea; CGI lead checks practical implications. Review the revised work against the agreed note before closing it.

    Close the discussion

    “Tell us what the viewer discovers at the beginning and understands by the end. Which questions need resolving before this becomes a production plan?”

    13 / Next decisions

    Develop the creative work.
    Then settle the build.

    During creative development

    Agree the visual approach.

    Refine the idea wording. Choose room direction, shot connections and styleframe moments. Agree channels and formats before locking the board. Test pacing and identify production questions.

    Later stages · as planned

    Research and plan production.

    Verify product dimensions, support orientation, wood, finish and construction. Develop the CGI method and tests. Agree budget, dates, capacity, output specifications and review rounds.

    NowStoryboard + look development→LaterProduct research + technical plan + boundaries→ThenProduction approval + build

    Before production: resolve the facts and decisions that affect the scene. Check final claims, product identification, credits and any required permissions before public release.

    Earlier generated concept frames are exploration, not approved product geometry. Separate abstract or optical-opening experiments are not added to this brief.

    Leave with an assignment

    “The next handoff is the board, three styleframes, still compositions and an open-question list. We’ll review those before committing to full production.”

    14 / Presenting this brief

    Explain the reason.
    Show the product. Discuss the work.

    The document gives the team the task. The briefing helps everyone understand it.

    Start with the job.

    Explain who we want to reach and what they should understand. Then show how the idea answers that job.

    Make the direction visible.

    Use the actual table photograph and reference lessons. Walk through the reveal rather than reading every line aloud.

    Check understanding.

    Ask the team to explain the intended story back in their own words. Record questions, decisions and next owners.
